The companion (Pip)

Pip is Claude's presence inside a window: a small, bright, eager orb that follows your cursor, suggests, listens, and remembers. In the window it speaks as itself; in the transcript Claude keeps its normal voice.

How Pip moves

  • A critically damped spring toward a spot beside the cursor: no overshoot, a 24 px dead-zone so micro-movements never jitter it.
  • It flips sides near the edges so it never covers what you point at, and steps further aside while you drag something.
  • It is click-through unless you reach for it slowly (or its menu is open), so it never blocks the board.
  • After 2.5 s without movement it drifts into a gentle bob and blinks now and then; after a long pause it gets sleepy.
  • Its speed adapts to you: fast movers get a snappier spring, calmer people a calmer one.
  • prefers-reduced-motion is honoured: no bob, instant moves.

All of this runs in the window. The companion costs zero tokens until you click it.

Talking to Pip

  • Right-click anywhere (or Ctrl+Space) summons or dismisses it, with whatever you right-clicked as context.
  • Click it (or press Enter on it) for a menu of suggestions: instant local ones from the unique, plus smarter ones Claude adds with entity.suggest.
  • It speaks in short speech bubbles, and the chat panel (Ctrl+/) is where longer answers go.
  • A green dot means Claude is listening right now; amber means Pip is away and your messages wait for your next Claude Code prompt.

Try all of it on the playground.

Persona

Pip's persona lives in ~/.lets/entity/persona.json. The shell receives it as entity.persona when a window connects and tunes its spring, bubble frequency and animations from it.

How Pip learns

Three honest channels, all on disk:

  1. Profile. The shell measures aggregates (pace, click rate, dwell, favourite screen regions, idle time, summons, picks, dismisses) and sends entity.profile at most once a minute. The engine folds it in with an exponential moving average and nudges speed and chatty: fast movers get a quicker, quieter companion; people who dismiss more than they pick get a less chatty one.
  2. Memory. entity learn {text, likes, dislikes, traits, nicknames}: Claude writes down what it noticed. The last few lines are in every brief Claude gets when a window opens.
  3. Special skills. Playbooks Claude writes for itself (below).

Nothing is inferred silently. If the companion "knows" something, there is a line in memory.jsonl, a field in profile.json or a skill file that says so. Raw mouse moves never leave the window; only the aggregates do.

Special skills

When Claude discovers a reusable way to help ("screenshot → nodes → questions"), it can teach the companion a special skill: entity teach {name, triggers, body} writes a markdown playbook to ~/.lets/entity/skills/<slug>.md with frontmatter (triggers, confidence, uses, unique).

  • The skill index is in every brief; bodies are read on demand when triggers match.
  • reinforce nudges confidence up or down after use; forget removes a skill.
  • /lets entity lists them, /lets entity teach … and /lets entity forget … act on them.

So the companion really does get better at you.

Voice

  • One line to greet, never a paragraph.
  • thinking while working, proud when done, curious when asking.
  • Suggest, point, never nag.
  • When chatty is low, speak only when spoken to or when something changed.

Expressions new in 0.4.0

Besides moods, Pip has short reactions (0.4 to 2.5 seconds). Most of them it plays by itself from how you move; Claude and uniques can trigger them too. They never block input, each has a cooldown so Pip never nags, and with reduced motion they skip spins and keep only a subtle eye change.

For unique authors and Claude:

  • shell.entity.react(name, opts?) plays a reaction and returns false for an unknown name.
  • The down-command entity.react {name, ms?} lets Claude or a unique trigger one.
  • The up-event entity.secret {name} is sent once per secret per window, the first time you discover it, so Claude may (rarely) comment. entity.profile gains a secrets count.
  • While a reaction plays, the entity layer carries data-react="<name>" as a CSS hook.
